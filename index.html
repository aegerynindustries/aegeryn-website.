01 / The problem

Searches are limited
by people, not drones.

Emergency agencies already fly drones with thermal cameras and AI. But every drone still needs its own pilot, every agency runs its own picture, and the hardest places to search have no GPS or signal. Adding more drones doesn't help if there's no one free to fly them.

01 / Pilots are the bottleneck

One drone, one pilot.

Most response is still one trained pilot per drone. Many units rely on volunteers, so the number of pilots, not drones, caps how much ground a search can cover.

02 / No shared picture

Agencies work apart.

Police, SES, fire, surf and marine rescue each bring their own equipment and maps. Joining them up during a search takes radio calls and time.

03 / The hardest places

No signal, no sky.

Gorges, caves, collapsed buildings and mangroves block GPS, radio and the view from above. At sea, the person keeps drifting away from where they were last seen.

Why now
  • Drones with AI already save lives.In June 2026 a Fire and Rescue NSW drone with AI and thermal imaging found two overdue hikers near Jindabyne at night.Source: FRNSW ↗
  • Agencies are building fleets.South Australia is investing $2 million in nine new SES drones, bringing its fleet to about 50, with thermal, loudspeakers and AI recognition.Source: SA Government ↗
  • Coordination is still in the lab.Swarm algorithms for drones and ground robots, and coordination of drone fleets, were presented as research and development at a 2026 emergency-services technology forum.Source: NSSN ↗
02 / The AEGIS platform

One operator.
Every robot. Every agency.

AEGIS is the software that turns drones, ground robots and boats into one coordinated search team. At its centre is AEGIS Command: one shared mission picture that any agency can join, running on the equipment they already own, with our own scouts for the places other drones can't reach.

  • AEGIS CommandOne shared mission picture at the centre
  • Air scoutsSwarm search with thermal and visual cameras
  • Ground scoutCaves, tunnels and collapsed buildings
  • Surface scoutCoasts, river deltas and mangroves
  • Relay nodesA mesh where radio fails
  • Agency dronesFleets agencies already own
  • Starlink · 4G/5G · meshWhichever link is up
  • AgenciesPolice, SES, fire, surf and marine rescue
AEGIS handles the busywork
  • Flying, take-off, return to base and battery swaps
  • Splitting the area and re-planning when a drone drops out
  • Keeping radio links alive and staying with a person once found
  • Ranking detections so operators aren't buried in false alarms
People make every decision

  • Confirming every possible find
  • Deciding who goes in, when and how
  • Taking manual control at any moment

Less piloting. Not less human judgment.

03 / Proven in simulation

Not just a vision.
Working software.

The AEGIS mission software is built and tested: a multi-drone search engine with coverage tracking, two allocation algorithms, a primary and backup mission coordinator, and automatic failure recovery. We then put it up against real-world limits: batteries, radio range, false alarms and people who keep moving. Every figure below is measured over 40 runs per setting.

Phase 1 · Core search and recovery
3.6×faster to cover a search sector with six scouts than with one: 14.9 minutes down to 4.2
100%of the sector still searched after losing three of six drones mid-mission, in 40 of 40 runs
40/40missions completed when the ground coordinator failed, because a backup on a drone took over. Without it: 2 of 40
−21%search time when six scouts launch from the middle of the area, as from a mothership: 4.2 to 3.3 minutes
Phase 2 · Real-world limits

Tested against the things that break real missions.

Drones now run on batteries and swap them, talk over radios with limited range that lose messages, raise false alarms that a person must check, and search for people who keep walking. The software still finds everyone, and the results show exactly where the next gains are.

13%of flying time out of radio contact at 400 m range with mesh radio and one relay drone. With direct links only: 55%
40/40missions found both people with repeat search passes, even when they kept walking. With a single pass: 28 of 40
4.8 minmedian time to find both people with every limit switched on at once, and both found in all 40 missions
94%of one operator's time spent reviewing alerts in an area full of false alarms. A second operator cut time to find from 7.1 to 4.1 minutes
Phase 2 · Battery planning

Keeping found people in view on short batteries.

When every drone launches together, their batteries run low together, and a person who has been found can be left with no drone overhead. AEGIS now keeps a charged spare at base to step in, sends drones to swap batteries before a trip too short to be useful, and, once everyone is found, swaps the lowest battery early so a fresh drone is always ready. Tested on a larger 1800 × 1200 m sector.

96%of the time a found person stayed in view of a drone with 15-minute batteries and battery-aware planning. Before: 67.5%
88%in view with 10-minute batteries when seven drones keep one as a spare. All seven searching: 46%
0batteries run flat across all 810 battery-planning test runs, because every drone heads home with a reserve
2×longer to find both people with 10-minute batteries when one of only six drones is held back as a spare: 5.9 to 12.0 minutes. A spare works best as an extra drone

      05 / The ground scout

      Where drones
      can't fly.

      AEGIS Rescue adds a small tracked ground robot for caves, tunnels and collapsed buildings. It goes in ahead of responders, maps the space as it moves, leaves a trail of radio relays so it never loses contact, and lets an operator talk to anyone it finds. Rescue teams decide what happens next.

      The problemHow the ground scout handles it

      No GPS underground

      Builds its own map with lidar and cameras as it moves, so every find comes with a position rescuers can follow.

      Radio can't pass through rock or concrete

      Drops small relay nodes behind it to form a chain back to the entrance. An air scout overhead links that chain to the operator.

      Rubble, stairs and narrow gaps

      A low, tracked body with flippers, designed to be small enough to get through voids people can't.

      Dark, dust and smoke

      Thermal camera, lights and microphones help it find people it can't see clearly.

      Unsafe air for responders

      Gas sensors report oxygen, carbon monoxide and flammable gases before anyone goes in.

      Someone trapped and frightened

      Two-way audio lets an operator talk to them, reassure them and learn how they're hurt.

      Cave and collapsed-structure rescues are led by trained specialist teams. The ground scout's job is to look first, so people don't have to go into an unknown space blind.

      07 / Drift planner

      Search where
      they'll be.

      Click the water to set where someone was last seen. Then change the time since and the current. The planner follows the drift through the water, along the coast and up the channels, for a range of possible currents. It covers every likely drift path back to the last-seen point, grows the area as uncertainty builds, and lays out a search pattern for each scout. It uses a simplified model for this website, not an operational planning tool.

        09 / Works with everything

        Seamless by design.
        Open to everyone.

        A search can involve five agencies with five kinds of equipment. AEGIS brings them together in one smooth, well-organised system: quick to set up, clear at a glance and dependable when it matters. A shared core does the thinking, and small adapters connect each system, so no agency is locked in.

        13 / Principles

        Built to be
        trusted.

        Search and rescue is about people at their most vulnerable. These commitments shape every part of AEGIS.

        01

        Human authority

        People confirm every find and make every rescue decision. Manual control is always one action away.

        02

        Life-saving use only

        AEGIS is built to find and protect people. It is not designed or offered for weapons or targeting.

        03

        Open by design

        Works with the equipment agencies already own, through open standards and adapters. No lock-in.

        04

        Privacy first

        No facial recognition. Video and locations are shared only with the people who need them.

        05

        Sovereign and accountable

        Australian-designed, data kept in Australia, and every action recorded in a mission log.

        06

        Evidence over hype

        We publish what we have measured and say clearly what is still a concept.
