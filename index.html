AEGERYN / Project Aegis

Find missing people faster across wide areas.

Aegis is a drone system for search and rescue. It scans a search zone with thermal and visual cameras, flags where a person may be, and sends each detection to a trained operator who decides what happens next.

In development. Not yet in field use.
See how it works

How Aegis works

One drone covers a zone in planned passes. Each step produces something a person can check.

01 Scan

Fly a planned pattern

The drone covers the search zone row by row, so every part of the area is visited and any gaps are visible on the map.

02 Detect

Look for person-shaped heat and shape

Thermal and visual models flag possible people. Each detection gets a location, a confidence score, and an image for review.

03 Report

Hand each detection to a person

Detections reach the operator with time and position. The operator checks each one and decides whether to send a ground team.

Design principles

These shape what Aegis does and what it does not do.

Detect, do not identify.
Aegis finds possible people by heat and shape. It does not identify anyone, and it does not use facial recognition.
People make the decisions.
A trained operator reviews every detection before any action. The drone never dispatches anyone on its own.
Measure before claiming.
Performance is reported as measured precision, recall, and false alarms per hour, under stated conditions.
Fly within the rules.
Field operations will follow CASA requirements and approvals before any flight outside a controlled test.

Where the project stands

Aegis is built in stages. Each stage has to work before the next one starts.

  1. Simulation Current focus. One drone over a simulated search zone, with detection and reporting.
  2. Bench tests Sensors and detection running on flight hardware, with the drone not flying.
  3. Tethered flight Basic flight and failsafes tested with the drone secured.
  4. Supervised field trials Short, approved flights with a trained spotter present.
  5. Partner pilots Trials alongside an emergency or volunteer search group, on their procedures.

After single-drone operation, the plan adds coordination across several drones, with a mesh network and a ground station.
